1. To be: ser o estar

Usamos to be para decir quiénes somos, cómo estamos o dónde estamos: I am a student (soy estudiante), She is happy (ella está contenta).

  • Afirmativa: persona + am / is / are.
  • Negativa: persona + am / is / are + not.
  • Interrogativa: Am / Is / Are + persona + resto de la frase + ? No añadimos do ni does.
To be en presente: afirmativa, negativa e interrogativa
PersonaAfirmativaNegativaInterrogativa
II am happy.I am not happy.Am I happy?
YouYou are happy.You are not happy.Are you happy?
HeHe is happy.He is not happy.Is he happy?
SheShe is happy.She is not happy.Is she happy?
ItIt is small.It is not small.Is it small?
WeWe are happy.We are not happy.Are we happy?
TheyThey are happy.They are not happy.Are they happy?

Formas cortas: I’m, you’re, he’s, she’s, it’s, we’re, they’re. En negativo: isn’t = is not, aren’t = are not y I’m not = I am not.

Respuestas cortas: Are you happy? — Yes, I am. / No, I’m not. Is she happy? — Yes, she is. / No, she isn’t. No decimos Yes, she’s como respuesta corta.

2. To have: tener

Para hablar de lo que tenemos usamos have o has. Con he, she, it usamos has; con las demás personas, have.

  • Afirmativa: I have a bike. / She has a bike. Tengo una bicicleta. / Ella tiene una bicicleta.
  • Negativa: persona + do not / does not + have.
  • Interrogativa: Do / Does + persona + have + resto de la frase + ?

PersonaAfirmativaNegativaInterrogativa
II have a bike.I do not have a bike.Do I have a bike?
YouYou have a bike.You do not have a bike.Do you have a bike?
HeHe has a bike.He does not have a bike.Does he have a bike?
SheShe has a bike.She does not have a bike.Does she have a bike?
ItIt has a tail.It does not have a tail.Does it have a tail?
WeWe have a bike.We do not have a bike.Do we have a bike?
TheyThey have a bike.They do not have a bike.Do they have a bike?

Recuerda: don’t = do not y doesn’t = does not. Después de does / doesn’t siempre escribimos have, no has.

Respuestas cortas: Do you have a bike? — Yes, I do. / No, I don’t. Does she have a bike? — Yes, she does. / No, she doesn’t.

También puedes decir have got

Para expresar posesión, especialmente en inglés británico, I have got a bike significa lo mismo que I have a bike. La pregunta y la negativa cambian: no mezcles las dos estructuras.

Have got para expresar posesión
PersonasAfirmativaNegativaInterrogativa
I / you / we / theyYou have got a bike.You haven’t got a bike.Have you got a bike?
He / she / itShe has got a bike.She hasn’t got a bike.Has she got a bike?

I’ve got = I have got; she’s got = she has got; haven’t = have not; hasn’t = has not. Respuestas cortas: Have you got a bike? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven’t. Has she got a bike? — Yes, she has. / No, she hasn’t.

3. Los posesivos: ¿de quién es?

Los adjetivos posesivos acompañan a un nombre: This is my book (este es mi libro). Los pronombres posesivos sustituyen al nombre: This book is mine (este libro es mío). No escribimos mine book.

De la persona al posesivo
PersonaAdjetivo + nombrePronombreSignificado
Imy bookminemi libro / mío
Youyour bookyourstu libro / tuyo; vuestro libro / vuestro
Hehis bookhissu libro / suyo (de él)
Sheher bookherssu libro / suyo (de ella)
Itits tail—su cola (del animal); no practicamos un pronombre independiente para it
Weour bookoursnuestro libro / nuestro
Theytheir booktheirssu libro / suyo (de ellos o ellas)
  • Los posesivos no cambian según el número de objetos: my book / my books; the book is mine / the books are mine.
  • His tiene la misma forma delante del nombre y solo: his pen / the pen is his.
  • No llevan apóstrofo: yours, hers, ours, theirs. Its indica posesión; it’s puede significar it is o it has.
